## 3. Mathematical Proof of Systemic Inconsistency

### A. Non-Ergodic Wealth Concentration
Consider an economy where individual wealth \(x_i(t)\) follows geometric Brownian motion with reallocation rate \(J\):
\[
dx_i(t) = x_i(t) \left( \mu \, dt + \sigma \, dW_i(t) \right) + J (\langle x \rangle - x_i(t)) \, dt
\]
When reallocation rate \(J \to 0\) or becomes negative (as Berman, Peters, and Adamou found in US empirical data since the 1980s), the time-average growth rate for almost all individual trajectories converges to:
\[
g_{\text{time}} = \mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2} < g_{\text{ensemble}} = \mu
\]
This mathematically proves that without continuous, aggressive structural reallocation, wealth concentration is an inevitable physical property of multiplicative economic dynamics.
