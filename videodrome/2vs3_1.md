## 3. The Mathematics of Systemic Singularity: \(r > g\) and Finite Substrates

Both `2.md` and `3.md` converge on the core mathematical contradiction of modern financial capitalism:

### A. The Exponential Flow vs. Finite Substrate Equation
Let \(K(t)\) represent total financial claims (capital stock) and \(Y(t)\) represent real biophysical output.
If financial capital compounds at rate \(r\) and real economic growth occurs at rate \(g\):
\[
K(t) = K_0 e^{rt}, \quad Y(t) = Y_0 e^{gt}
\]
When \(r > g\), the ratio of financial claims to real output grows exponentially:
\[
\frac{K(t)}{Y(t)} = \frac{K_0}{Y_0} e^{(r - g)t} \xrightarrow{t \to \infty} \infty
\]
Because real output \(Y(t)\) is ultimately bounded by the finite biophysical substrate (\(K_{\text{nature}} \ge K_{\min}\)), the financial claims must either be periodically wiped out through hyper-inflation, debt defaults, and systemic crashes, or enforced through ever-increasing extraction from labor and natural resources.
